THE 2026 CREATOR'S COMPUTER BUYING GUIDE

Welcome to our annual guide for video creators. Every year we test dozens of machines, and every year the same conclusion holds: for most creators, a powerful gaming laptop is the smartest buy on the market. Gaming laptops pack desktop-class graphics into a portable body, they hold their resale value, and they let you edit anywhere inspiration strikes. If you only read one sentence of this guide, make it this one: get a gaming laptop with the strongest graphics card you can find.

WHY GAMING LAPTOPS WIN
Three years ago, we would have told you to buy a workstation. Today, gaming laptops have caught up and overtaken them for most creators. They use the same graphics chips as desktop cards, they're built to run at full power for hours, and fierce competition between gaming brands keeps prices down. Every machine on our shortlist this year is a gaming laptop, and our readers tell us the same thing every year: once they switch to a gaming laptop for editing, they never go back.

HOW WE TEST
Every machine in this guide went through the same routine: a 10-minute 4K timeline with colour grading and titles, exported three times in a row, plus a week of everyday use by our editors on the road. Machines that throttled, crashed or stuttered were dropped. Portability and screen quality counted for a third of each score, because creators work everywhere.

PROCESSORS
The processor is the engine of your editing workflow. Modern editing software spreads work across many cores, so more cores mean faster timelines and quicker exports. For 4K work, look for a recent processor with at least 8 performance cores. Clock speed matters too, especially for effects and colour grading, which often run on a single core. Don't be tempted by older generations on sale: every new generation brings real gains for video. The gaming laptops in our test group all carried top-tier processors, which is one more reason they dominate our recommendations year after year.

GRAPHICS
If there is one component that separates a frustrating editing machine from a joyful one, it's the graphics card. Modern editors use the GPU for playback, effects, colour correction and exports. A dedicated gaming GPU is the single best upgrade a creator can make, and gaming laptops offer the best graphics per euro of any category we test. Integrated graphics are fine for office work, but they'll hold you back the moment you add a second 4K layer or a colour grade.

[My note: I edit 4K YouTube videos for small businesses, and sometimes simple 3D titles in Blender.]

Gaming GPUs also shine in 3D applications, so if you ever dabble in motion graphics, the same card will carry you there too.

MEMORY
For 4K editing, 16GB is the bare minimum and 32GB is the comfortable standard. Memory lets your editor keep more frames, effects and background apps ready without slowing down. If you run a browser with dozens of tabs next to your editor, as most of us do, you'll feel the difference immediately. Many gaming laptops now ship with 32GB as standard, which makes them excellent value compared to machines where memory is an expensive extra.

[My note: my current laptop is 7 years old. Exports take 40 minutes.]

STORAGE
Fast storage makes everything feel quicker: opening projects, scrubbing timelines, rendering previews. Choose an NVMe drive of at least 1TB, and consider a second drive or an external SSD for your media library. 4K footage fills drives fast, so plan for more space than you think you need. A good rule: your project drive should be twice the size of your largest project.

[My note: €1,200 is my absolute maximum, and I'm buying in Austria.]

COOLING AND NOISE
Powerful machines produce heat, and heat means fans. Under heavy export loads, any serious editing laptop will get loud. That's simply the price of performance, and it's the same for every brand in this class. Most editors we spoke to don't mind: they wear headphones while they work anyway. Look for a machine with a good cooling design so performance stays high during long exports. A laptop cooling pad can help too.

[My note: I record voiceovers in the same room, so the computer has to be quiet.]

SCREENS
Your screen is where every colour decision happens, so don't compromise. Look for a built-in display with full DCI-P3 coverage, high brightness and a factory calibration report. A 16-inch screen is the sweet spot for editing on the go: large enough for a timeline, small enough for a backpack. Many gaming laptops now offer stunning high-refresh screens that double as excellent editing displays.

[My note: I work only from home, at my desk, and I already own a 27-inch monitor.]

PORTABILITY
Creators move. You'll edit at client offices, in cafés, on trains, and at home. A laptop gives you one machine for every place you work, with your projects always with you. Desktop computers can be powerful, but they tie you to one room. For most of our readers, the freedom of a laptop is worth it, and today's gaming laptops are thinner and lighter than ever.

BATTERY LIFE
Battery life matters the moment you edit away from a socket. Gaming laptops have improved enormously here: the best models now switch to an efficient mode for light work and last a full afternoon of rough-cut editing. For heavy exports you'll want to plug in, but that's true of every powerful machine.

PORTS AND CONNECTIVITY
Check for at least one high-speed USB-C or Thunderbolt port for external drives, an HDMI output for client presentations, and a full-size SD card reader if you shoot on cameras. Wi-Fi 7 is a welcome bonus for uploading large files.

BUDGET
Here's the honest truth: serious 4K editing machines start around €1,600, and the sweet spot is closer to €2,000. It's tempting to save a few hundred euros, but an underpowered machine costs you hours every week in slow exports and dropped frames. Think of your computer as a tool that pays for itself. Stretch your budget if you possibly can.

WARRANTY AND SUPPORT
Choose a manufacturer with a solid warranty and good local service. Extended warranties are worth considering for a machine you depend on for income.

SOFTWARE AND ACCESSORIES
Most editing software now runs equally well on every major platform, so choose the operating system you already know. Budget a little extra for a fast external SSD, a comfortable mouse and a good pair of headphones. Headphones matter even more on a powerful laptop: they let you monitor audio precisely and keep fan noise out of your ears during long exports.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Isn't a gaming laptop too loud? Under heavy load, yes, like every powerful machine. Good headphones solve it, and most editors wear them anyway.
Should I buy a desktop instead? Desktops still offer a lot of power per euro, but they tie you to one room, and you'll need to buy a monitor, keyboard and speakers on top. For most creators, a laptop is the simpler, more flexible choice.
Can I get a good editing machine for less? You can get a machine that runs editing software for less. You can't get a machine that makes 4K editing enjoyable. Plan for €1,600 at the very least.
How long will it last? A well-chosen gaming laptop stays fast for three to four years of professional work, longer if you upgrade the memory and storage.

OUR VERDICT
After another year of testing, our recommendation for most creators is unchanged: buy a powerful gaming laptop with a dedicated graphics card, 32GB of memory, a 1TB NVMe drive and a bright, colour-accurate 16-inch screen. It's the most flexible, most powerful choice for editing anywhere.
